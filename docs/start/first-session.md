# Your first study session

Start with one question you want to be able to answer confidently. You can practise
from uploaded notes or begin with a topic alone.

**You’ll need:** an open PreparationPalAI workspace and an available model.
If you don’t have those yet, [get access first](access.md).

## 1. Choose a small topic

Open **Preparation Coach** from the sidebar. In **Topic, subject, or syllabus focus**,
be more specific than the name of a whole subject.

| Too broad for a first session | A useful starting point |
| --- | --- |
| Physics | Newton’s second law, force diagrams, and units |
| Biology | Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium and heterozygote frequency |
| Computer science | Binary search invariants and boundary cases |
| Chemistry | Limiting reagents and molar ratios |

These are examples you can adapt to your own course or interview requirements.

## 2. Pick Learn & recap

Choose **Learn & recap**, a manageable question count, and an appropriate difficulty.
For a first session, try **3 or 5 questions** at **easy** or **medium** difficulty.

Choose **Mixed** if you want a combination of question styles. Choose a written
format when explaining the method matters more than recognising an answer.

## 3. Add your notes, if you have them

Open **Knowledge base** in the top bar and upload a relevant PDF or image. Wait
until ingestion completes before starting your session.

You can also start without documents. The coach will label that session
**General model knowledge**. With uploaded material, it displays the contributing
sources so you can compare the output with your notes.

## 4. Answer before revealing feedback

For a written answer, show your steps, assumptions, and units. Set **Confidence**
to reflect how sure you were *before* the answer was graded, then select **Submit answer**.

For example, instead of only writing “6 N”, explain the law you used, substitute
the values, and check the unit. The purpose is to practise your reasoning, not
to guess what wording the model expects.

## 5. Use the feedback

Read the feedback and open **Reference answer and solution**. For written answers,
you may see separate reasoning, accuracy, and clarity scores.

- If you missed a concept, restate it in your own words before moving on.
- If the feedback seems wrong, check your notes or another trusted reference.
- In recap mode, the next question may adapt to a concept you just missed.

## 6. Review your results

At the end, open **View results**. Pick one weak topic from the revision plan.
Select **Practise weak areas**, ask Study Agent to explain it, or make a flashcard
for an idea you want to recall later.

Recent coach sessions appear under **Your recent sessions**. You can return to a
saved session or revisit its results.

Model scores are practice feedback, not official exam marks.
