# Prepare for an exam

Use your syllabus and notes to generate questions, try a timed session, and review
the topics you missed.

## Before the session: choose a slice of the syllabus

Upload a relevant chapter, lecture notes, or a revision sheet. Choose one learning
objective rather than the whole subject. For example:

> Apply the first law to a closed system, with sign conventions and units.

Keep your syllabus beside you. The app can generate questions, but it cannot know
which topics your examiner will emphasise unless your material and prompt say so.

## First pass: learn without a clock

In **Preparation Coach**, choose **Learn & recap**. Use a short set of questions
and explain your answers before reading the reference.

When you miss a question, identify the cause:

| What happened? | A useful next action |
| --- | --- |
| Forgot a definition or relationship | Make one focused flashcard |
| Used the wrong assumption or method | Ask Study Agent for a contrast or counterexample |
| Made a numerical or unit error | Work through a similar problem with every step shown |
| Recognised an answer but couldn’t explain it | Switch to a written question format |

## Second pass: practise under constraints

Choose **Exam practice**, **Mixed** question format, and a realistic time limit.
Use your course’s allowed resources and calculator rules as your own practice
conditions. The app does not enforce exam-board rules for you.

Answer the question you see, state necessary assumptions, and move on when a
problem is taking too much of the session. Unanswered questions count as zero.

## After the session: review missed topics

Read the topic breakdown and choose **Practise weak areas**. Check questionable
feedback against the original material before treating it as a gap in your knowledge.

At your next study session, begin with one previously missed idea before adding a
new topic. A lower score on unfamiliar questions can be more informative than a
perfect retake immediately after reading the solutions.

!!! tip "For calculation-heavy subjects"
    Prefer written answers for derivations and multi-step reasoning. Check units,
    rounding, assumptions, and the meaning of the final result yourself. Generated
    solutions are not an official marking scheme.
